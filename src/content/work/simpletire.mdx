Buying tires online asks people to make a confident decision about a complex product they rarely think about. SimpleTire sells at high volume, so every bit of friction in that decision costs real carts. I reworked the flow to take the friction out.

## The problem

Tire selection was cumbersome, the design had aged, and conversions were sliding. The brief was to design new features, build prototypes worth testing, and lift the experience without losing the brand.

## Research first

I used both sides of the research toolkit: interviews, surveys, analytics, and usability testing. Together they showed where customers and stakeholders hit friction, and gave each design decision something real to stand on rather than a hunch.

## Where the work landed

Most of the gains came from the moments closest to the purchase:

- **Product list and page** rebuilt around clearer layouts, real filtering, structured specs, reviews, and interactive galleries
- **Offers and rebates** pulled into an accessible deals section with clear actions and personalised picks
- **Product comparison** as a side-by-side tool for specs, pricing, and reviews, so the choice is informed
- **Cart and installation** joined up, so buying tires and booking the appointment happen in one flow
- **Helper prompts** offering contextual support, maintenance tips, and recommendations along the way

## Outcome

The reworked flow lifted user engagement by roughly 20 percent, with conversion and satisfaction moving up alongside it. The work was later featured in a published Toptal case study.
