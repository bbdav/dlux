The same HVAC manufacturer behind the controller app had a website working against it. The job was to turn a dense, technical site into one where a buyer can find the right product and ask for a quote without friction.

## What was in the way

The old site fought its visitors: auto-playing slideshows, menus that wrapped and confused, navigation repeated in three places, and content written for search engines and engineers rather than the people trying to buy.

<Figure
  src={oldsite}
  alt="The previous corporate website before the redesign."
  caption="Before: autoplay slideshows, redundant navigation, and SEO-first copy."
/>

## Listening before drawing

I started with stakeholder alignment across product, engineering, and marketing to pin down the goals, the real audiences, and the constraints. Then I sat with users, in person and over Zoom, watching them complete tasks and thinking aloud, to learn how they actually navigate and decide.

<Figure
  src={selector}
  alt="An early whiteboard sketch of the product selector."
  caption="The product selector, worked out at the whiteboard before anything else."
/>

## Mapping the path

Proto-personas captured who we were designing for. A detailed customer journey map laid out their goals, expectations, and the emotional highs and lows, which made the pain points impossible to ignore. From there I built an information architecture that mapped real pathways through the site, not just a sitemap.

<Figure
  src={journey}
  alt="The customer journey map for the website redesign."
  caption="A journey map turned scattered frustration into a list of things to fix."
  width="wide"
/>

## Structure, then surface

Wireframes and interactive Figma prototypes put logical flow and intuitive navigation first, so stakeholders could react to something real and iterate fast. A documented style guide kept the visual language consistent as it scaled across the site.

<Figure
  src={wireframe}
  alt="A landing page wireframe prioritising flow and navigation."
  caption="Wireframes focused on flow first, so feedback landed on structure, not colour."
/>

## Outcome

The new site brought a significant drop in bounce rate. Users called out the streamlined navigation, the content that was finally easy to scan, faster product searches, and a presentation that matched the company behind it.
