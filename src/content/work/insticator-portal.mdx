Publishers came to the portal to answer one question: where is my revenue coming from? The old interface made that surprisingly hard. The redesign put the answer up front.

## Where it broke down

The data publishers needed most, revenue by content type, was scattered across a wall of cards. Anything beyond the surface meant leaving the main view, which broke their train of thought. The portal held the numbers but never told the story.

The brief was to rebuild the navigation and the reports around how revenue actually works, while keeping the legacy production data reachable through the transition.

## Moving the navigation

Competitor analysis pointed to a clear pattern: the industry had moved toward drawer-style menus. I mapped a new information architecture so the team agreed on the menu before any pixels moved, then relocated navigation from the top to a vertical left sidebar that collapses to icons. That handed the right side of the screen back to the content.

## Reading revenue at a glance

Three compact cards sit at the top of every report, rolling revenue drivers up by day, week, month, and quarter. Below them, the reports do the heavy lifting: stacked bars for additive data, multi-line charts where metrics live in different units, and consistent Revenue, Unit Load RPM, and Unit Loads tabs so the layout never surprises you.

## The small things that get used most

The date picker gets touched on nearly every visit, so it earned real attention. Presets like Yesterday and Last 7 days cover the common cases, and built-in comparison against the previous period or last year answers the next question before it is asked.

## Outcome

The work shipped as more than screens: presentations to leadership, close coordination with engineering, and interviews with the team during the build. What landed was a professional, lightly playful portal backed by a reusable component library, where the path from question to answer is short.
